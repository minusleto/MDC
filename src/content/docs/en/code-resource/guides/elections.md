Everything below is a breakdown of `common/scripted_effects/00_subideology_scripted_effects.txt` — the plumbing effects that calculate party strength, run elections, and decide who the AI forms a coalition with. Useful if you're rebalancing parties, adding a new subideology, or trying to understand why the AI picked a particular coalition.

## The update pipeline

Three "master effects" chain-call everything else:

| Master effect | When it runs | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `startup_politics` | Once, on country/game start | Full init: party totals → positions → election threshold → majority threshold → coalition strength → party name → top 2 |
| `recalculate_party` | On any party popularity change (add_popularity etc.) | Light recalc: totals → positions → coalition strength |
| `hold_elections` | On election day | Full electoral recalc, including the threshold cutoff and ban flags |
| `recalculate_party_strength` | Periodically (non-election) | Same as `hold_elections` but skips ban flags and doesn't refresh the top 2 |

## How a subideology splits its ideology group's weight

`add_up_party_totals` is the heart of the system. Key mechanic: each subideology (`party_pop_array^0..23`) is normalized **within its own ideology group** (democratic/communism/fascism/neutrality/nationalist — 0–3 / 4–9 / 10–11 / 12–19 / 20–23), then scaled by that group's current vanilla popularity (`party_popularity@democratic` etc. — the same slider from base HOI4). So the sum of every subideology in a group always equals the vanilla group percentage — moving the group slider via effects does nothing unless you also move a specific subideology's share within it.

If a group's vanilla popularity is >1% but all its subideologies somehow sum to <1% (a data mismatch), the engine **randomly** hands 1% to one of the group's subideologies (`random_list`, equal weights) so the group doesn't vanish from the chart. The Shia subideologies (8, 9) are excluded from that lottery unless the country has `has_idea = shia`.

## Election threshold and majority threshold

- `election_threshold` defaults to **5%** (`setup_election_threshold`, if the variable isn't set yet), changed via the `modify_election_threshold` effect, clamped to **0–10%**.
- Parties below the threshold get zeroed out in `party_pop_elect_array` — they can't participate in elections/coalitions even if they're actually popular in `party_pop_array`.
- `majority_threshold` = 50% of the sum of **all** electorally-counted parties (`update_majority_threshold`) — recalculated every time before an election.

## Party ranking and the top 2

`set_party_positions` sorts all 24 subideologies by descending popularity and stores the indices in `pos_array` (0 = most popular). `find_two_highest_parties` finds the two race leaders (`max_index_1`, `max_index_2`) — these become the "seeds" the AI uses to build a coalition (see below).

## Party bans — a numbering trap

⚠️ **Important**: the ban flags are **1-indexed** (`party1_banned` … `party24_banned`), while the subideology array is **0-indexed** (`party_pop_array^0` … `^23`). So:

| Flag | Zeroes out index |
|---|---|
| `party1_banned` | `party_pop_elect_array^0` (Pro-Western Autocrats) |
| `party2_banned` | `party_pop_elect_array^1` (Conservatives) |
| ... | ... |
| `party24_banned` | `party_pop_elect_array^23` (Monarchists) |

If you set `set_country_flag = party12_banned` meaning to ban the party at `party_index = 12` (Moderate Islamists) — that's wrong, it'll actually zero out index 11 (Salafi Jihadism) instead. The flag you want is `party13_banned`.

## Cost of inviting a party into the coalition

`refund_government_coalition` computes the political cost (in national-power points) of inviting a specific party: for each current coalition member, it takes the political distance to the candidate (see the matrix below), multiplies by the candidate's weight, sums it all up, and multiplies the total by **1500**.

## How the AI builds a coalition

`AI_setup_government` builds **two** candidate coalitions — one seeded around the race leader (`max_index_1`), one around the runner-up (`max_index_2`) — and picks whichever is cheaper:

1. `make_affinity_arrays` — for each of the two seeds, computes the political distance to every other party (via the matrix below)
2. `reduce_affinity_array_party_1/2` — greedily adds the **closest** (lowest-distance) candidates one at a time until the combined electoral vote exceeds `majority_threshold`; after the first time it overshoots, it backs out the last addition and tops up with one final pick instead
3. Whichever candidate coalition ends up with the lower total cost (`cost1` vs `cost2`) wins

## Political distance between parties

552 directional values (0.1 = near-allies, 1.0 = maximally incompatible). **Not symmetric** — of 276 unique pairs, only 138 have matching values in both directions; most pairs have a different "cost to invite" depending on which direction you look at it. Example: the Military (22) can bring Salafi Jihadism (11) into a coalition almost for free (0.2), while Salafi Jihadism considers the Military maximally alien (1.0) — the reverse isn't reciprocated.

Row = who's forming the coalition (`max_index_1`/`max_index_2`), column = who's being considered as a candidate.

## Ready-made utility: shift one party's popularity

`add_relative_party_popularity` is a reusable function, documented right in the source, syntax:

```
set_temp_variable = { party_index = X }              # party index, 0-23
set_temp_variable = { party_popularity_increase = Y } # fraction, e.g. 0.02 = +2%
set_temp_variable = { temp_outlook_increase = Z }     # optional, also a fraction
add_relative_party_popularity = yes
```

## Puppets inherit their overlord's ruling party

`effect_puppet_match_ruling_party_to_overlord` (used in puppet scope, with `FROM` = the overlord) copies the overlord's ruling party and coalition onto the puppet 1:1, and also sets a country flag `set_<subideology>` for whichever specific party it is (e.g. `set_conservatism`, `set_Western_Autocracy`) — presumably used elsewhere to trigger something related to falling in line with the metropole's politics.
